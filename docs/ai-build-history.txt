     Two architectural simplifications that buy most of the speed

     1. Gemini accepts PDFs natively. Results filings, annual reports, and
        concall transcripts go
        straight to the model as PDF bytes. This deletes the entire
        pdfplumber/OCR pipeline.
     2. Every source is plain HTTP JSON or HTML. Combined with (1), there is no
        reason to run a
        Python worker. One TypeScript toolchain, one npm install.

     ---

     Product point of view

     ▎ An investor's inbox should be a triage queue, not a feed.

     Most submissions will be "dashboard + chatbot." FINN's differentiator is
     that it is opinionated
     about what you should not read. Three surfaces, all mobile-first.

     1. The Brief — windowed digest (Push flavour 1)

     Default window 24h, with a picker (24h / 7d / 30d / custom). Every
     portfolio announcement in the
     window is scored and bucketed:

     - ACT — material, needs a decision today
     - WATCH — relevant, read when you can
     - NOISE — collapsed to a single line: 41 routine filings hidden ▸

     Burying the noise visibly is the product statement. Each ACT/WATCH card
     carries: company,
     category chip, a one-line "why it matters" (a judgment, not a summary), and
     a source chip that
     opens the actual filing PDF.

     Two enriched card types, exactly as the brief calls out:
     - Results filing → auto-extracted Revenue / EBITDA / PBT / PAT table with
       QoQ and YoY deltas.
     - Management change → a short background on the incoming person (push-side
       enrichment from
       outside sources is explicitly allowed by the brief).

     2. Signals — triggered alerts (Push flavour 2)

     User-configurable thresholds, the point being the investor decides what 
     "unusual" means:
     - volume > N× trailing 20-day average (default 2×)
     - absolute price move beyond ±X% in a day (default 4%)
     - "always alert me on these categories" (results, M&A, management_change,
       …)

     Rendered as a compact rules sheet, with fired alerts injected inline at the
     top of The Brief.

     3. Dossier — grounded research (Pull)

     Enter any NSE ticker → research grounded only in annual reports + concall
     transcripts.
     For the fastest-shippable cut, build two views exceptionally well rather
     than eight thinly:

     - Promised vs Delivered ← the signature feature. The brief says this is
       "the kind of thing we
       value most." Extract management's explicit guidance from the last ~4
       concall transcripts, pair
       each promise with what the subsequent results actually showed, and render
       a ledger:
       promise → horizon → actual → ✅ Kept / ⚠️ Partial / ❌ Missed, each row
       expanding to the
       verbatim quote and a link to the source page.
     - Snapshot — compact business + financial header (what they do, segment
       mix, headline numbers,
       multi-year trajectory sparkline) so the ledger has context.
     - (stretch, cheap — one extra call over already-extracted material) Bull vs
       Bear, two tight cards.

     Anti-slop discipline: nothing renders without a citation chip. The Pull
     extractor only ever
     sees retrieved primary-source text, and its system prompt forbids outside
     knowledge, news, and
     broker commentary — enforced structurally, not just asked for.

  What I built

  The brief's hard problem isn't aggregation, it's filtering. So FINN is built
  around a claim most dashboards avoid making: most filings don't matter, and 
  the app should say so out loud.

  Three surfaces, all mobile-first:

  - The Brief (push digest) — every filing across an 18-stock portfolio in a
    window you choose, triaged into Act / Watch / Buried. Routine filings
    collapse to a single line that tells you how much was hidden. Nothing is
    deleted, and tapping any score shows exactly which rules fired — triage is
    auditable, not a black box. Results filings render Revenue/EBITDA/PBT/PAT
    with QoQ and YoY; management changes render who's arriving and where from.
  - Signals (triggered alerts) — volume above N× its 20-day average, price
    beyond ±X%, plus per-category rules. You move the sliders and the list
    recomputes as you drag; the point is that you define "unusual", not me.
  - Research (pull) — any NSE symbol, grounded strictly in annual reports and
    concall transcripts.

  The bit I'd point you at

  Promised vs Delivered. FINN extracts every explicit forward-looking commitment
  from management's past earnings calls, then adjudicates each against the most
  recent call and the company's own reported figures. Reliance comes out 6 kept
  / 6 missed — the battery and solar capacity timelines committed to in Jan and
  Jul 2026 have all slipped to "late next year", in management's own words.
  Deepak Nitrite is 3 kept / 7 missed.

  Every row expands to two verbatim quotes — the promise and the evidence — each
  linking to the source PDF. Where the documents don't settle a question the
  verdict is OPEN; the prompt is explicit that abstaining beats guessing. KPIT's
  entire ledger is OPEN, correctly, because its guidance is FY27/FY29 targets
  that haven't matured.

  And the quotes are checked, not trusted. npm run audit re-extracts every cited
  PDF and asserts each quote appears in it — currently 143/143. That check
  earned its keep immediately: the first run came back 143/148, catching five
  Asian Paints "evidence" quotes that were investor-deck phrasings the model had
  paraphrased into quote form. Right numbers, invented quotation marks. They're
  now dropped automatically at build time, and a verdict that loses its quote
  says so in the UI. That failure mode is exactly the slop you warned about, and
  it's invisible unless you test for it.

  Key decisions and trade-offs

  Ingestion is decoupled from serving. NSE and screener.in throttle datacenter
  IPs, so an app that scrapes on request is a demo that dies in the room. A
  worker runs on a residential IP and writes a committed 954KB JSON corpus; the
  deployed app only reads it. A fresh clone runs with no API key and no network.

  No Python. Every source turned out to be plain HTTP (Yahoo's chart endpoint is
  open JSON — no yfinance needed), and Gemini reads PDFs natively, which
  deleted the whole pdfplumber/OCR layer. One TypeScript toolchain, one npm 
  install.

  NSE's own taxonomy does most of the classification for free. Its desc field is
  a ~105-value vocabulary that maps cleanly onto your reference categories, so
  rules handle it at zero tokens and the LLM only sees what survives triage.

  Model routing, measured not asserted. Rules → flash-lite (bulk, batched
  25/call) → flash-lite (PDF→numbers) → flash + 8k thinking (adjudication only).
  Building the entire corpus — 438 filings enriched, 7 dossiers, 91 commitments
  adjudicated — took 57 live calls and ~$0.09 notional, and $0.00 actual on the
  free tier. A content-hash cache makes re-runs free. data/cost-report.json has
  the numbers.

  Two routing decisions worth flagging: Tier 3 was meant to be Pro, but every
  Pro model 429s on the free tier, so it's Flash with a raised thinking budget
  instead. And I demoted PDF extraction from flash to flash-lite on evidence —
  on a benchmark filing flash-lite returned Reliance's PAT as ₹29,550cr,
  matching the company's own press release, where flash returned ₹29,250cr and
  left the QoQ/YoY columns empty. Cheaper and better, so it won.
